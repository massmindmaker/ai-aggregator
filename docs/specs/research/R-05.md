> Research item for AIAG TMA. Scope: legal/compliance shape of the crypto side.

> Author: staff-eng research pass. Date: 2026-06-03.

## 1. Executive summary

The crypto side of AIAG TMA sits on top of **three independent legal walls**, and the hardest one is not what the product context assumed:

1. **App-store wall (the real blocker).** Telegram's own published policy — not just Apple/Google — **mandates Telegram Stars (XTR) for *digital* goods/services sold inside a Mini App on mobile.** Crypto, TON, USDT, and any third-party payment provider are **explicitly prohibited for digital goods**, and the enforcement lever is Telegram itself: it will **hide your bot/Mini App from mobile users** if you sell digital goods in any currency other than Stars. AI-agent inference credits are textbook "digital goods." So the canon assumption — "TMA currency = crypto credits (USDT/TON), TON-Connect-only" — **directly conflicts with Telegram's ToS for the mobile surface.** This is the single most important finding and changes the product, not just the legal entity.

2. **RF wall (259-FZ).** A Russian person/entity (resident ≥183 days/12mo) **may not accept cryptocurrency as payment for goods/services** (259-FZ Art. 14). Holding crypto as *property* is legal; *accepting it as consideration* is not. So all crypto inflow/outflow must live on a **foreign entity** — which the canon already says. The IT-tax cooperative split is a *separate* RF concern (rubles only) and does not solve, nor is it touched by, the crypto problem.

3. **Foreign-entity / facilitator wall.** Holding USDC and running an x402 facilitator is mostly an unregulated-merchant activity *if* AIAG is the payer/customer of compute, but the moment AIAG **holds user balances denominated in USDT and lets users spend them**, that starts to look like custody / e-money / VASP activity in many jurisdictions, which is where a license question appears.

**Bottom line for the founder:** the crypto-credits-in-TMA model as currently drawn is **not shippable on iOS/Android via the Telegram mobile client without violating Telegram ToS.** There are 2–3 viable structures (Section 7), but each forces a product decision: use Stars on mobile (give up ~30% + the crypto rail there), restrict crypto to an external web surface, or accept being a hidden/desktop-only Mini App. The foreign entity is necessary but not sufficient.

## 2. App Store / Google Play / Telegram policy for digital credits in a Mini App

### 2.1 The actual rule (this is the crux)

- Apple told Telegram that **paying for digital goods/services without Apple IAP violates App Review Guidelines 4.7 and 3.1.1.** Telegram's response was to create **Telegram Stars** and, **as of 2024 (the "June 12" cutover), require that all payments for digital goods/services in bots and Mini Apps be made exclusively in Telegram Stars.** ([Telegram Stars blog](https://telegram.org/blog/telegram-stars), [Bot Payments API for Digital Goods](https://core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars))
- Telegram's payments doc states it plainly (paraphrased from the live page): *"Payments for digital goods and services must be carried out exclusively in Telegram Stars,"* and to the direct question of whether crypto/other currencies are allowed: *"No. All payments via bots and mini apps for digital goods and services inside Telegram apps must exclusively use Telegram Stars."* ([core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars](https://core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars))
- **Enforcement is by Telegram, not (only) Apple/Google:** *"Telegram cannot display your bot or mini-app to mobile users if you attempt to sell digital goods and services via other currencies or through third-party payment providers."* So the failure mode is **invisibility on mobile**, plus risk of bot ban. ([core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars](https://core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars))
- The policy explicitly maps to **Apple 3.1.1 / 3.1.1(a) / 3.1.3(b)** and **Google Play Payments Policies**. ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/))

### 2.2 What counts as "digital goods"

Telegram/legal commentary lists digital goods as: **subscriptions, in-game currencies, extra levels, extended playing time, premium channel access, VIP status, virtual items.** ([Aurum — TMA Legal Checklist 2025](https://aurum.law/newsroom/Telegram-Mini-App-Legal-Checklist-in-2025)) **AI inference credits / "agent run credits" are unambiguously digital goods** — the same category as in-game currency. There is no realistic argument that LLM credits are "physical goods or offline services."

### 2.3 The physical-goods carve-out (does not help us)

- *"If you're selling physical goods or services, you may use different currencies or payment providers"* — Telegram permits third-party crypto gateways (USDT, near-zero fees) **only for physical goods and offline services.** ([core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars](https://core.telegram.org/bots/payments-stars), [PayRam — Stablecoin micro-economies in TMA](https://payram.com/blog/accept-stablecoin-payments-telegram-mini-apps))
- AIAG sells inference. This carve-out **does not apply.**

### 2.4 TON Connect is NOT a loophole for digital goods

- Multiple sources are explicit: *"Using an external link to accept crypto for these items violates Telegram's Terms of Service and can result in your bot being banned,"* and *"Direct TON payments via TON Connect … applies to physical goods and services, not digital goods consumed within the app."* ([OmiSoft — Monetise a TMA 2026](https://omisoft.net/gb/blog/how-to-monetize-telegram-mini-app/), [Merge — How to build a TMA](https://merge.rocks/blog/how-to-build-a-telegram-mini-app-your-telegram-mini-apps-guide))
- The product context's note "TON-Connect-only is viable" is **incorrect for digital goods on mobile.** TON Connect is allowed for wallet connection and for physical-goods payments; it is not a sanctioned path to sell inference credits on the mobile client.

### 2.5 Additional TON-blockchain mandate

- Since early 2025, **any Mini App that creates or distributes tokens/blockchain assets must do so exclusively on TON**; assets on Ethereum/BNB face removal. Wallet connection must use **TON Connect SDK only.** ([Aurum](https://aurum.law/newsroom/Telegram-Mini-App-Legal-Checklist-in-2025)) This is relevant only if AIAG ever mints a credit token; if credits stay an off-chain ledger balance, this mandate is dormant but worth noting for any future tokenization.

### 2.6 The grey-zone reality (how crypto Mini Apps actually run)

- Crypto casinos / iGaming Mini Apps **do** run USDT economies — by treating the Mini App as **a web app and pushing payment to an external browser surface / off-Telegram flow**, accepting that this **violates Telegram ToS for the mobile surface** and risking de-listing. ([Value The Markets — Telegram mini-app crypto gambling](https://www.valuethemarkets.com/igaming/the-silent-surge-how-telegrams-mini-app-ecosystem-is-unleashing-the-next-wave-of-crypto-gambling)) This is a tolerated-until-it-isn't posture, not a compliant one, and that vertical also routinely gets bots banned and is a magnet for scam-enforcement sweeps. ([Dataconomy — TMA abused in crypto scam campaign, May 2026](https://dataconomy.com/2026/05/04/telegram-mini-apps-abused-in-large-scale-crypto-scam-campaign/))
- **Concrete rejection/removal risk:** the practical risk is **(a)** Telegram hiding the Mini App entry-point on iOS/Android (silent, immediate, no appeal SLA), and **(b)** bot ban for repeated digital-goods-in-crypto violations. It is **less** an Apple-review rejection (the Telegram host app is already approved) and **more** a Telegram-platform enforcement action. This distinction matters: you are not fighting Apple review, you are fighting Telegram's automated/manual platform moderation, which is opaque and fast.

## 3. Russian law — 259-FZ, 161-FZ, DFA rules

### 3.1 259-FZ "On Digital Financial Assets and Digital Currency" — the core ban

- **Holding is legal; accepting as payment is not.** Crypto is classified as **имущество (property)**, not money. Russian persons may buy, sell, and hold it as an investment/asset. ([259-FZ on Consultant](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_358753/), [pravo163.ru — crypto regulation 2025](https://pravo163.ru/pravovoe-regulirovanie-kriptovalyut-v-rossii-zakony-ogranicheniya-i-perspektivy-2025/))
- **Art. 14 prohibition (load-bearing):** *"Юридические лица … и физические лица, находящиеся в РФ не менее 183 дней в течение 12 … месяцев, не вправе принимать цифровую валюту в качестве встречного предоставления за передаваемые товары, выполняемые работы, оказываемые услуги."* — RF residents **may not accept digital currency as consideration for goods/works/services.** Advertising crypto as a payment method is likewise barred. ([Art. 14 — Consultant](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_358753/176562423610280f8b2578ab3480887bf7707562/))
- **Penalties** (per commentary, confirm current КоАП text): individuals 100k–200k ₽; legal entities **700k–1,000,000 ₽**; plus **confiscation of the crypto used unlawfully.** ([pravo163.ru](https://pravo163.ru/pravovoe-regulirovanie-kriptovalyut-v-rossii-zakony-ogranicheniya-i-perspektivy-2025/))
- **Mining** (since 1 Nov 2024): registered entities/IEs must register with ФНС; individuals capped at 6,000 kWh/month without registration. Not directly relevant to AIAG (we don't mine), but signals direction of travel. ([pravo163.ru](https://pravo163.ru/pravovoe-regulirovanie-kriptovalyut-v-rossii-zakony-ogranicheniya-i-perspektivy-2025/))
- **ЭПР (Experimental Legal Regime), since 1 Sep 2024:** cross-border *foreign-trade* settlements in crypto are permitted **only inside a controlled CBR experimental framework**, and access is gated to "super-qualified" investors (≥100M ₽ capital or ≥50M ₽ annual income). This is **not a path for a small startup** to legitimize crypto receipts. ([pravo163.ru](https://pravo163.ru/pravovoe-regulirovanie-kriptovalyut-v-rossii-zakony-ogranicheniya-i-perspektivy-2025/))
- **2026 development to watch:** the Constitutional Court ruling of **20 Jan 2026** found part 6 of Art. 14 unconstitutional insofar as it blocked judicial protection of claims tied to lawful possession/use of crypto obtained by non-mining means — i.e., **courts must now protect crypto-property rights**, but this **does not** lift the payment-acceptance ban. ([259-FZ ed. 15.12.2025, Kontur.Normativ](https://normativ.kontur.ru/document?moduleId=1&documentId=503403))

**Net:** Any flow where a Russian-resident person/entity *receives crypto in exchange for AIAG services* is illegal. The **foreign entity is mandatory** for the crypto leg. This is consistent with the canon ("ALL crypto exposure off the RF entity").

### 3.2 161-FZ "On the National Payment System" — e-money / payment-operator angle

- 161-FZ governs **money-transfer operators, electronic-money operators, payment-system operators,** etc. Issuing **"electronic money" (ЭДС)** or operating a payment system in RF requires being a licensed credit/payment institution under CBR supervision. ([161-FZ — Bank of Russia](https://www.cbr.ru/PSystem/acts/161-fz/), [Art. 3 definitions](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_115625/4f41fe599ce341751e4e34dc50a4b676674c1416/))
- **Why it matters for AIAG:** a **prepaid USDT-denominated "credit balance" that users top up and spend** can be characterized as an e-money / stored-value instrument. **If issued from the RF entity in rubles, 161-FZ e-money licensing risk appears.** Keeping the credit ledger on the **foreign entity and denominating it in a foreign unit** keeps it outside 161-FZ's RF perimeter — but then RF marketing of that balance to RF users is the exposure to manage. Recent amendment **248-FZ (23 Jul 2025, effective 1 Sep 2026)** tightens NPS rules further — flag for re-check before launch. ([161-FZ ed. 09.04.2026 — Kontur.Normativ](https://normativ.kontur.ru/document?moduleId=1&documentId=505641))
- **Practical read:** structure the AIAG credit as a **prepaid usage allowance for the foreign entity's SaaS**, not as transferable/withdrawable "money." Non-withdrawable, non-transferable, single-merchant credits are far easier to defend as "prepaid service" rather than "electronic money" or a "payment instrument."

### 3.3 152-FZ "On Personal Data" — localization (relevant because TMA serves RU users)

- Since **1 July 2025** (amendments by **23-FZ of 28.02.2025**): **primary processing (collection, recording, systematization, storage) of RF citizens' personal data must occur on servers physically in Russia**; using foreign databases for *initial collection* of RF-citizen PD is prohibited. ([comply.ru](https://comply.ru/tpost/c43ezsout1-lokalizatsiya-i-transgranichnaya-peredac), [Lidings legal update](https://www.lidings.com/ru/media/legalupdates/localization_pd_update/))
- **Cross-border transfer** of data that was *first* collected into an RF-located database is **not additionally restricted** per RKN clarification 08-134789 (24.03.2025) / Mincifry П25-44929 (12.05.2025) — i.e., **collect locally first, then you may mirror abroad.** ([comply.ru](https://comply.ru/tpost/c43ezsout1-lokalizatsiya-i-transgranichnaya-peredac))
- **Fines:** legal entity **1–6M ₽**; repeat within a year **6–18M ₽.** ([rkn-help.ru](https://www.rkn-help.ru/help/lokalizaciya-personalnyh-dannyh-v-rf-novye-trebovaniya-i-proverki-s-2025-goda))
- **Tension with the foreign-entity model:** if the **foreign entity** collects RF users' PD (Telegram ID, email, wallet) **directly onto a foreign VPS first**, that violates the localization-first rule. **Mitigation:** the RF entity (or an RF-located DB) does the *primary* collection/storage of RF-user PD, and the foreign entity processes a **pseudonymized/derived** copy for billing. The current single 2GB foreign VPS holding RF-user records would be **non-compliant** as drawn. (Note: enforcement against a purely foreign operator with no RF presence is weak in practice, but the RF cooperative gives RKN a domestic target — see risk in Section 8.)

## 4. Foreign entity for holding USDC + running an x402 facilitator

### 4.1 What x402 is and what the facilitator does

- **x402** = Coinbase's open standard reviving HTTP 402 Payment Required for **internet-native stablecoin payments** (APIs, apps, AI agents). A **facilitator** verifies a signed payment payload and settles it on-chain; it is "the trust layer." ([Coinbase — Introducing x402](https://www.coinbase.com/developer-platform/discover/launches/x402), [x402 whitepaper](https://www.x402.org/x402-whitepaper.pdf), [Sherlock — x402 explained](https://sherlock.xyz/post/x402-explained-the-http-402-payment-protocol))
- **You do not have to self-host the facilitator.** Coinbase Developer Platform runs a hosted facilitator (USDC + ERC-20 on Base/Polygon/Arbitrum/World/Solana; **free tier 1,000 tx/month**). Stripe also added x402 USDC-on-Base support. **Self-hosting the facilitator is a choice that increases your money-handling footprint** (and likely your licensing exposure), not a requirement. ([Coinbase x402 docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/welcome), [Circle — autonomous payments with USDC + x402](https://www.circle.com/blog/autonomous-payments-using-circle-wallets-usdc-and-x402), [The Block — Stripe x402 on Base](https://www.theblock.co/post/389352/stripe-adds-x402-integration-usdc-agent-payments))

### 4.2 Two very different roles — which one is AIAG?

This distinction drives the entire licensing question:

- **Role A — Payer/customer (low regulatory weight).** AIAG holds USDC in its own treasury wallet and **pays** external tools/compute via x402 as a *buyer*. This is ordinary corporate crypto-treasury use. Most jurisdictions treat paying your own bills in stablecoin as **not a regulated money-services activity.** A self-hosted facilitator used **only for your own outbound payments** is closer to a payment SDK than a money-transmitter.
- **Role B — Holding user balances / moving third-party money (high regulatory weight).** AIAG holds **users' USDT** as a spendable balance, and/or runs a facilitator that **settles other people's payments.** This is where **VASP / money-transmission / e-money / custody licensing** is triggered in nearly every regime. If users can top up, hold, and withdraw, you are arguably operating a wallet/custody + payment service.

**The product as drawn is Role B** (users hold USDT credits). To stay near Role A, you must make credits **prepaid, non-withdrawable, non-transferable, single-merchant SaaS allowance** (see 3.2). That framing is the difference between "merchant selling SaaS in stablecoin" and "unlicensed e-money issuer."

### 4.3 Jurisdiction options for the foreign entity

| Jurisdiction | Posture | Tax | License reality for AIAG | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UAE / Dubai (VARA)** | Crypto-native regulator, "tax-free" headline | 0% personal; 9% corp >AED 375k | VARA license needed for VASP activity; **merchant-accepting-stablecoin-for-own-SaaS may not need full VASP** | Strong if you want a real operating base + banking; setup cost & substance requirements are real. ([KuCoin — Top crypto jurisdictions 2026](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/top-5-crypto-jurisdictions-in-2026-from-estonia-to-seychelles)) |
| **Estonia** | EU, reputable, MiCA on-ramp | 0% on retained profit (Estonian model) | **VASP→MiCA CASP transition required; old VASP valid only to 1 Jul 2026.** Min capital €100k for CASP. | EU passporting attractive, but **MiCA CASP is heavy** for a startup. Overkill if you're a merchant, not a CASP. ([B2Broker](https://b2broker.com/news/getting-a-crypto-exchange-license-in-estonia-and-other-regions/), [Eternity Law](https://www.eternitylaw.com/news/estonian-cryptocurrency-exchange-license/)) |
| **Lithuania** | EU, fast, crypto-friendly, 5AMLD | EU standard | Same MiCA CASP deadline pressure | Faster than Estonia historically. ([GoFaizen-Sherle](https://gofaizen-sherle.com/crypto-license)) |
| **BVI / Cayman / Seychelles** | Offshore, light-touch | 0% | No license if purely merchant/treasury; VASP regimes exist but lighter | Banking & payment-rail access is the hard part; reputational/UX friction with users & partners. - **EU-wide constraint:** **MiCA** — VASP licenses under old national frameworks are valid **only until 1 July 2026**; firms must move to **MiCA CASP authorization** to keep operating in the EU. If AIAG is *not* a CASP (just a merchant), MiCA CASP is unnecessary — but you must be confident the user-balance model doesn't tip you into "custody/exchange." ([KuCoin — 2026 deadline](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/top-5-crypto-jurisdictions-in-2026-from-estonia-to-seychelles))

### 4.4 Key insight

**Whether AIAG needs a crypto license is a function of product design, not geography.** Design credits as non-withdrawable prepaid SaaS (Role A-ish) and most jurisdictions let you operate as an ordinary merchant accepting stablecoin. Let users hold/withdraw balances (Role B) and you need VASP/e-money/custody licensing almost everywhere. **Pick the product model first, then the jurisdiction.**

## 5. The RF entity split (ИП → production cooperative, 152-FZ)

This is a **separate, ruble-only concern** — it governs the **WEB aggregator** (ai-aggregator.ru, ₽ only), not the crypto leg. It is included because the brief asks and because the two entities interact via 152-FZ.

### 5.1 Why move off ИП

- **IT preferential 7.6% insurance contributions are NOT available to ИП** — only to *organizations* with Mincifry accreditation and ≥70% IT-revenue share. ([Kontur.Extern — IT insurance contributions](https://www.kontur-extern.ru/info/83605-strahovye_vznosy_dlya_it_kompaniy), [Elba — IT taxation 2025](https://kontur.ru/elba/spravka/79556-nalogooblozhenie_it_kompaniy))
- **The 7.6% rate is changing:** from **2026 the IT preferential rate rises from 7.6% to 15% within the unified base limit** (7.6% only above the limit). IT still keeps **5% corporate income tax** and **VAT exemption** (VAT exemption survived to second reading). ([GARANT — IT contributions change from 2026](https://www.garant.ru/news/1906337/), [e-kontur — IT taxes from 2026](https://e-kontur.ru/blog/16132/it-s-2026-goda))
- **ИП burden:** every ИП pays **fixed contributions "for themselves" (~53,658 ₽ in 2025 + 1% over 300k ₽)** regardless of activity — a structural cost an org avoids. ([taxcoach](https://www.taxcoach.ru/taxbook/Vozmognosti_vedeniya_biznesa_cherez_proizvodstvennyy_kooperativ))

### 5.2 Production cooperative (ПК) — the proposed target

- A **производственный кооператив** is sometimes used for **lawful insurance-contribution optimization**, but the mechanism is narrow and contested: **profit distributed by reason of personal labor participation is subject to NDFL *and* insurance contributions** (no saving there); only **dividend-style distribution from net profit (not for labor) escapes contributions** (NDFL 13–15% only). ([osnova.capital — ПК: panacea or not?](https://osnova.capital/proizvodstvennyy-kooperativ-panaceya-dlya-ekonomii-strahovyh-vznosov-ili-net/), [taxcoach](https://www.taxcoach.ru/taxbook/Vozmognosti_vedeniya_biznesa_cherez_proizvodstvennyy_kooperativ))
- **Reality check:** the contribution-saving via ПК is **aggressive and FNS-scrutinized**; combined with **IT accreditation (7.6%→15%, 5% profit tax)** the marginal benefit of a ПK over an ООО-with-IT-accreditation may be small and higher-risk. **An ООО + Mincifry IT accreditation is the conventional, lower-risk path** to the IT tax regime. The ПK is an *optimization play*, not a compliance necessity.

### 5.3 152-FZ interaction (the real reason both entities matter together)

- The RF entity is what gives AIAG a **lawful place to do localization-first PD collection** for RF users (Section 3.3). The foreign entity should **not** be the primary collector of RF-user PD. So the split is: **RF entity = rubles + RF-user PD primary store; foreign entity = crypto treasury + foreign-denominated SaaS credit ledger over pseudonymized data.**

## 6. Constraints summary (the walls, restated)

1. **Telegram ToS:** digital goods on mobile = **Stars only**; crypto/TON/USDT/3rd-party = prohibited; penalty = **hidden on mobile + bot-ban risk.** Hard wall.
2. **259-FZ Art. 14:** RF resident person/entity **cannot accept crypto** as payment. Hard wall → forces foreign entity for crypto.
3. **161-FZ:** RF-issued ruble "credits/e-money" risk payment-operator licensing → keep stored-value off RF entity, make it non-withdrawable prepaid SaaS.
4. **152-FZ:** RF-user PD must be **collected/stored in RF first** → foreign VPS cannot be the primary PD store for RF users; current single 2GB foreign VPS is non-compliant as drawn.
5. **MiCA / VASP:** user-held, withdrawable balances ⇒ licensing almost everywhere; old EU VASP dies **1 Jul 2026**.
6. **Self-hosted x402 facilitator** increases money-handling footprint; **hosted facilitator (Coinbase/Stripe/Circle)** is lower-risk and likely sufficient.

## 7. Two-to-three viable structures (tradeoffs)

### Structure A — "Compliant mobile: Stars on mobile, crypto on web" (lowest platform risk)
- **Mobile Telegram client:** sell credits **only in Telegram Stars** (Stars→Toncoin withdrawal via Fragment lets you realize value in crypto downstream). Fully ToS-compliant; visible on iOS/Android.
- **Crypto top-up (USDT/TON via TON Connect / external web):** offered **only on the standalone web app / desktop / outside the Telegram mobile surface**, where Telegram's digital-goods-Stars rule does not gate visibility the same way.
- **Entities:** foreign entity holds USDC treasury, sells SaaS credits (non-withdrawable prepaid), uses **hosted x402** for outbound tool/compute payments; RF entity handles ₽ (web aggregator) + RF-user PD localization.
- **Tradeoffs:** (+) shippable on mobile today, low ban risk; (+) keeps crypto rail for power users on web. (−) pay Telegram's Stars cut (Apple/Google ~30% flows into Star price) on the mobile rail; (−) two payment UIs; (−) Stars↔crypto bridge adds accounting complexity.

### Structure B — "Crypto-native, web/desktop-first, mobile-thin" (max crypto, accept platform limits)
- **Crypto credits (USDT/TON) are the primary currency**, but the **Telegram mobile Mini App is positioned as a thin client** that does **not** sell digital goods in-client; purchase happens on the **external web app**. Accept that the mobile Mini App may be **de-prioritized/hidden** for purchase flows and lean on desktop + web + bot-driven external links.
- **Entities:** same foreign/RF split as A; **strongly consider hosted x402** to avoid facilitator-as-MSB exposure; design credits as non-withdrawable to dodge VASP.
- **Tradeoffs:** (+) full crypto economics, no Stars cut; (+) matches the founder's crypto-credits vision. (−) **persistent ToS-violation risk on mobile** → silent de-listing/bot ban with no SLA; (−) worse mobile UX (the whole point of a TMA is the mobile surface); (−) this is the grey-zone posture crypto-casinos use — durable only until enforcement.

### Structure C — "Dual-rail by surface, single ledger" (pragmatic hybrid, recommended to evaluate)
- **One internal credit ledger** denominated in a neutral unit (e.g., "AIAG credits" pegged to USD/USDT), **funded by two rails:** Stars on mobile, USDT/TON on web/desktop. Mobile users buy credits with Stars; web users buy the same credits with stablecoin. The credit is a **non-withdrawable prepaid SaaS allowance** (defuses 161-FZ/VASP).
- **Entities:** foreign entity issues credits + holds treasury + uses hosted x402 outbound; RF entity = ₽ web + RF-user PD store; BYOK/own-provider stays free (no debit) per existing code rule.
- **Tradeoffs:** (+) compliant on mobile *and* crypto-native on web with one billing model; (+) cleanest accounting; (+) credits-as-prepaid-SaaS minimizes licensing. (−) most engineering (current code debits a ₽ `tg_user_balances` → must migrate to a neutral credit unit *and* add a Stars rail, not just a USDT rail); (−) Stars rail still carries the ~30% store cut on mobile.

**Cross-cutting recommendations for all three:**
- Prefer **hosted x402 facilitator** (Coinbase/Stripe/Circle) over self-hosting unless a lawyer confirms self-hosting stays in "Role A."
- Make AIAG credits **non-withdrawable, non-transferable, single-merchant prepaid SaaS** to stay out of e-money/VASP/161-FZ.
- Keep **RF-user PD primary collection on RF infra** (not the foreign 2GB VPS) for 152-FZ.
- Get **written legal opinions** in (a) RF (259/161/152-FZ) and (b) the chosen foreign jurisdiction before any user money moves.

## 8. Risks

- **R1 — Mobile invisibility / bot ban (HIGH).** Selling inference credits in crypto on the Telegram *mobile* client violates Telegram ToS; enforcement is silent de-listing on iOS/Android and/or bot ban. This is the dominant risk and it kills the mobile growth thesis if triggered.
- **R2 — 259-FZ confiscation + fine if any crypto touches the RF entity (HIGH).** 700k–1M ₽ for legal entities + confiscation. The foreign entity must be the *only* crypto-receiving party; sloppy commingling (e.g., RF ИП wallet) is the failure mode.
- **R3 — VASP/e-money reclassification (MEDIUM-HIGH).** Withdrawable/transferable user balances tip AIAG from "merchant" to "money-services" → licensing in foreign jurisdiction and/or 161-FZ exposure if any ruble e-money is issued from RF.
- **R4 — 152-FZ localization breach (MEDIUM).** Foreign VPS as primary PD store for RF users = 1–6M ₽ (6–18M ₽ repeat). The RF cooperative gives RKN a domestic enforcement target it would otherwise lack.
- **R5 — Stars economics (MEDIUM).** Going Stars-compliant on mobile imports Apple/Google's ~30% IAP cut into the Star price + Telegram's own withdrawal mechanics (Fragment), compressing unit economics on the mobile rail.
- **R6 — MiCA / VASP-deadline drift (MEDIUM).** EU old-VASP sunset 1 Jul 2026; choosing an EU base means committing to CASP-grade compliance or confirming non-CASP merchant status.
- **R7 — Grey-zone enforcement sweeps (MEDIUM).** Crypto Mini Apps are a current target for scam/malware enforcement (May 2026 campaigns); even a legitimate crypto-credit Mini App can be caught in broad sweeps and brand-impersonation takedowns.

## Adversarial review

> Reviewer: adversarial verification pass. Date: 2026-06-03. Method: independent re-fetch of the load-bearing primary sources (Telegram payments-stars doc, 259-FZ Art. 14, КоАП crypto-penalty bill, 152-FZ localization, Stars economics) + attempts to refute the central thesis.

**Verdict: HOLDS UP (partly).** The two hard walls that drive the recommendation — (1) Telegram mandates Stars-only for digital goods on mobile and will hide the Mini App otherwise, and (2) RF residents may not accept crypto as consideration — are **independently re-confirmed against primary sources** and are correctly load-bearing. The memo's core conclusion (crypto-credits-in-TMA is not shippable on the Telegram mobile surface; foreign entity is necessary-but-not-sufficient; design credits as non-withdrawable prepaid SaaS) **stands.** But several supporting claims are over- or under-stated, one self-contradiction weakens R4, and the strongest counter-structure (the desktop economics carve-out) is buried. The recommendation needs tightening, not reversal.

### What re-verified cleanly (no change)
- **Telegram Stars-only mandate.** The live payments-stars doc verbatim: *"Payments for digital goods and services must be carried out exclusively in Telegram Stars"*; on crypto: *"No. All payments via bots and mini apps for digital goods and services inside Telegram apps must exclusively use Telegram Stars…"*; enforcement: *"Telegram cannot display your bot or mini-app to mobile users if you attempt to sell digital goods and services via other currencies or through third-party payment providers."* Section 2 is accurate. The TON-Connect-is-not-a-loophole point (2.4) is correct. **This is the crux and it holds.**
- **259-FZ Art. 14 acceptance ban.** Re-confirmed: RF legal entities + 183-day residents may not accept digital currency as consideration for goods/works/services, plus the Part 7 ban on *advertising* crypto as a payment method. Foreign entity is genuinely mandatory for the crypto leg.
- **TON-only blockchain mandate** (2.5) and the Feb-2025 migration deadline are real.

### Issues found (ordered by materiality)

1. **The КоАП penalty is presented as current law; it is a pending bill (MEDIUM error, well-hedged).** The memo cites "individuals 100k–200k ₽; legal entities 700k–1M ₽ + confiscation" for crypto-acceptance. Verification: those exact numbers come from a **July-2025 draft introducing a *new* КоАП Art. 15.49**, expected to bite **from 2026** — and Russian commentary is explicit that *"оплата… запрещена с 2021 года, но в июле 2025 отсутствует нормативная база реального наказания."* So the **ban is real and old (since 2021); the monetary-penalty + confiscation mechanism is new and not yet enacted as of this writing.** The memo's parenthetical "per commentary, confirm current КоАП text" partially saves it, but the Risk-R2 framing ("700k–1M ₽ + confiscation … HIGH") overstates *present* enforceability. Correct read: the *legal prohibition* is hard today; the *enforcement teeth* are arriving ~2026 and should be treated as imminent, not present. This actually **strengthens urgency** (the window to structure correctly is closing), so it does not change the recommendation — but the memo should not assert the fine as live law.

2. **Self-contradiction on 152-FZ extraterritoriality (MEDIUM).** Section 3.3 / R4 say the foreign VPS as primary PD store is non-compliant, then the §3.3 parenthetical undercuts itself: *"enforcement against a purely foreign operator with no RF presence is weak in practice."* Independent check refutes that hedge: **152-FZ is explicitly extraterritorial** — it binds a foreign operator that targets RF data subjects via a Russian-language interface, .ru domain, ruble payment, or RF-user-directed marketing. AIAG (ai-aggregator.ru, RU-language TMA, RF users) is squarely in scope **regardless of the RF cooperative's existence.** So the memo's own framing — "the RF cooperative gives RKN a domestic target it would otherwise lack" — is misleading: RKN already has jurisdiction; the cooperative mainly makes *collection* (fines, RU-bank blocking) easier, not jurisdiction itself. The localization risk is **higher and more independent of the entity split** than the memo implies. Tighten R4 upward and drop the "weak in practice" hedge.

3. **The desktop-Stars economics carve-out is under-weighted (MEDIUM — affects the recommendation).** R5 and Structure A/C all import "~30% Apple/Google cut" into Stars on mobile. Verification confirms ~32% effective on **mobile** — but also that **desktop/web Stars purchases are only ~3-4%** (no store IAP tax; just Fragment spread + exchange). The memo never surfaces this. Consequence: the "Stars cut kills mobile unit economics" framing is true *only for in-app mobile Star purchases.* A user who buys Stars on Telegram **Desktop/Web** (or via @PremiumBot / Fragment) and then spends them in the mobile Mini App pays ~3-4%, not 32%. This materially improves Structure A/C and is a legitimate optimization the memo omits. (Caveat: you cannot *force* mobile users to buy Stars on desktop, and steering them off-app may itself brush Telegram/Apple anti-steering rules — but presenting desktop top-up as the cheaper path is standard practice.)

4. **Stars 21-day hold + 1,000-Star withdrawal floor not modeled in treasury/cash-flow (MEDIUM, omission).** The Stars→Fragment→TON path (open question Q7, Structure A) carries a **21-day holding period before withdrawal** and a **1,000-Star minimum**. For a startup this is a real **working-capital / liquidity drag** on the mobile rail that the memo flags as "accounting complexity" but never sizes. Any Structure-A/C cash-flow model must assume mobile revenue is locked ~3 weeks before it becomes spendable TON. This was already noted in project memory ("Phase 15 — no Telegram Stars: 32% fee, 21d hold") — the memo should reconcile with that prior decision, which it does not mention.

5. **Structure B is softer-pedaled than the evidence warrants (LOW-MEDIUM).** B ("crypto-native, mobile-thin") is described as "durable until enforcement." The memo's *own* cited May-2026 scam-sweep sources + the "silent, no-appeal-SLA de-listing" mechanic make B closer to **actively hazardous** than "grey but durable." Given AIAG is a *legitimate* business (not iGaming), the asymmetry is bad: same enforcement exposure, none of the casino margins that make the risk worth it. B should be labeled **not recommended** rather than co-equal.

6. **Unexamined assumption: are AIAG credits even "digital goods," or could the agent be framed as a service the user's own funds/keys pay for?** The memo asserts (2.2) "AI inference credits are unambiguously digital goods" — correct for *AIAG-sold* credits. But the product already has a **BYOK / own-provider = free (no debit)** path (per project memory and existing code). In BYOK mode the user pays the upstream provider directly and AIAG sells *no digital good* — that flow is **outside** the Stars mandate entirely. The memo never connects this: BYOK is not just a billing edge case, it is a **partial compliance escape hatch** for the mobile surface (no AIAG-sold digital good ⇒ no Stars requirement for that user). Worth elevating.

7. **Minor / unverified-but-plausible:** ЭПР super-qualified thresholds (≥100M ₽ / ≥50M ₽), MiCA old-VASP sunset 1 Jul 2026, Estonia €100k CASP capital, and the ПК contribution mechanics were **not** independently re-verified in this pass; they are secondary to the recommendation and the memo's "confirm with counsel" disclaimer covers them. The 161-FZ prepaid-vs-e-money framing (3.2) is directionally sound but is the kind of characterization a regulator can contest — it is a lawyer question, correctly flagged as such.

### Tightened recommendation
1. **Adopt Structure C (dual-rail, single non-withdrawable USD-pegged credit ledger), with two corrections to the memo:**
   - Route Stars top-ups to **desktop/web where economically sensible** (~3-4% vs ~32%); model the **21-day hold + 1,000-Star floor** as a working-capital line, and reconcile with the prior "Phase 15 — no Stars" decision before committing engineering.
   - Treat **BYOK/own-provider as a first-class compliance lane** on mobile: those users buy no AIAG digital good, so the Stars mandate does not gate them. This preserves a crypto-adjacent, mobile-visible path for power users without ToS exposure.
2. **Demote Structure B to "not recommended"** — for a legitimate (non-iGaming) business the silent-de-listing downside dwarfs the upside.
3. **Re-characterize R2 and R4:**
   - R2 (259-FZ): the *acceptance ban* is HIGH and present; the *700k–1M ₽ + confiscation penalty* is a **2026 bill, not current law** — treat as imminent, structure now.
   - R4 (152-FZ): raise severity — localization is **extraterritorial and independent of the cooperative**; primary RF-user PD collection must be on RF infra regardless of entity choice. Do not rely on "foreign operator = weak enforcement."
4. **Keep:** foreign entity for the crypto leg (mandatory), hosted x402 over self-hosted (lower money-handling footprint), non-withdrawable/non-transferable single-merchant prepaid-SaaS credit design (the single most leverage-y compliance choice), and the two-jurisdiction legal-opinion gate before any user money moves.
5. **Add an open question Q8:** does Telegram's anti-steering / "don't direct users off-platform for digital-goods payment" rule constrain *suggesting* desktop Star top-up to mobile users? This determines how aggressively the desktop-economics carve-out (#3) can be marketed.

**Net:** the memo's spine is correct and well-evidenced; the recommendation survives adversarial attack. The deltas are: do not present the KoAP fine as live law, raise the 152-FZ severity (it's already in scope), surface the desktop-Stars economics and the BYOK compliance lane (both improve the chosen structure), and explicitly demote Structure B.
